## 1.1 Speaking German
Although there are a few unfamiliar sounds in German, the language is much more phonetic than English—meaning words are generally pronounced the way they are spelled. In Week 1, focus purely on the **sounds** and **spelling patterns** of the words.

---

## 1.2 The Alphabet and Spelling

### 1. The 26 Letters + 3 Umlauts
The German alphabet contains all 26 letters of the English alphabet, plus three vowels with an **umlaut** (`¨`). These represent completely distinct sounds from the base vowels:
* **ä / Ä**
* **ö / Ö**
* **ü / Ü**

### 2. The Letter *ß* (Eszett / Sharp S)
In German, a double *ss* sound is written with the letter **ß** (which **never** starts a word) in two specific situations:
1. After a **long vowel**
2. After a **vowel combination (diphthong)** like *ei*, *au*, or *äu*

If the preceding vowel is **short**, it is written with a double **ss**.

#### Compare *ss* (Short Vowel) vs. *ß* (Long Vowel / Diphthong)

| Short Vowel (*ss*) | Long Vowel / Diphthong (*ß*) | Example Sentence for Memory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Kuss** (kiss) | **Gruß** (greeting) | *Einen schönen **Gruß** und einen **Kuss**!* (A nice greeting and a kiss!) |
| **floss** (flowed) | **Floß** (raft) | *Das **Floß** **floss** auf dem Wasser.* (The raft floated/flowed on the water.) |
| **blass** (pale) | **fraß** (ate - animal) | *Der Hund **fraß** alles, das Kind war **blass**.* (The dog ate everything, the child was pale.) |
| **muss** (must) | **Fuß** (foot) | *Ich **muss** zu **Fuß** gehen.* (I must go on foot.) |
| — | **Fleiß** (diligence) | *Er arbeitet mit viel **Fleiß**.* (He works with great diligence.) |
| — | **Strauß** (bouquet / ostrich) | *Sie kauft einen **Strauß** Blumen.* (She buys a bouquet of flowers.) |
| — | **äußern** (to express) | *Darf ich einen Wunsch **äußern**?* (May I express a wish?) |

### 3. Capitalization Rules
German capitalization has three golden rules that differ from English:

1. **ALL Nouns Begin with a Capital Letter:** Not just proper names and places, but every single noun (people, objects, concepts).
   * *Examples:* **M**ann (man), **F**rau (woman), **H**aus (house), **T**isch (table), **Z**eitung (newspaper).
2. **Formal 'You' is Always Capitalized:** The pronoun **Sie** (formal 'you') and related formal possessives like **Ihr** ('your') always begin with a capital letter anywhere in a sentence.
3. **'I' is Lowercase:** Unlike English, the first-person singular pronoun **ich** ('I') starts with a lowercase letter (unless it is the very first word of a sentence).

#### Example Sentences Demonstrating Capitalization:
* **Ich** trinke **W**asser, und **Sie** trinken **K**affee.  
  *(**I** drink **w**ater, and **you** [formal] drink **c**offee.)*
* Wo ist **Ihr** **A**uto, **H**err **M**üller?  
  *(Where is **your** [formal] **c**ar, **M**r **M**üller?)*
* Heute kaufe **ich** ein **B**uch und eine **U**hr.  
  *(Today **I** am buying a **b**ook and a **c**lock.)*
