## 1.1 Speaking German

Although there are a few unfamiliar sounds in German, the language is much more phonetic than English—meaning words are generally pronounced the way they are spelled. In Week 1, focus purely on the **sounds** and **spelling patterns** of the words.

## 1.2 The Alphabet and Spelling

### 1. The 26 Letters + 3 Umlauts

The German alphabet contains all 26 letters of the English alphabet, plus three vowels with an **umlaut** (`¨`). These represent completely distinct sounds from the base vowels:

- **ä / Ä**
- **ö / Ö**
- **ü / Ü**

### 2. The Letter _ß_ (Eszett / Sharp S) - Simplified Rule

Both **ss** and **ß** make the exact same sharp "s" sound (like the _ss_ in _hiss_). The only difference is how long you hold the vowel sound right before it:

- **Use `ss` after a FAST, SHORT vowel:** If the vowel sound is quick and clipped, write `ss`. _(Example: Kuss [kiss] — quick u)._
- **Use `ß` after a SLOW, STRETCHED vowel:** If you stretch the vowel out, write `ß`. _(Example: Gruß [greeting] — stretched u)._
- **Use `ß` after a TWO-VOWEL combo (`ei`, `au`, `äu`):** Whenever two vowels team up, they are naturally long, so always use `ß`. _(Example: Fleiß [diligence])._
- **Rule of thumb:** A word can **never** start with **ß**.

### 3. Capitalization Rules

German capitalization has three golden rules that differ from English:

1. **ALL Nouns Begin with a Capital Letter:** Not just proper names and places, but every single noun (people, objects, concepts).
   - _Examples:_ **M**ann (man), **F**rau (woman), **H**aus (house), **T**isch (table), **Z**eitung (newspaper).
2. **Formal 'You' is Always Capitalized:** The pronoun **Sie** (formal 'you') and related formal possessives like **Ihr** ('your') always begin with a capital letter anywhere in a sentence.
3. **'I' is Lowercase:** Unlike English, the first-person singular pronoun **ich** ('I') starts with a lowercase letter (unless it is the very first word of a sentence).

#### Example Sentences Demonstrating Capitalization:

- **Ich** trinke **W**asser, und **Sie** trinken **K**affee.  
  _(**I** drink **w**ater, and **you** [formal] drink **c**offee.)_
- Wo ist **Ihr** **A**uto, **H**err **M**üller?  
  _(Where is **your** [formal] **c**ar, **M**r **M**üller?)_
- Heute kaufe **ich** ein **B**uch und eine **U**hr.  
  _(Today **I** am buying a **b**ook and a **c**lock.)_
