## 1.3 Pronunciation: Individual Letters

German spelling is much more consistent and phonetic than English. However, several letters change their sound depending on their position in a word, or are pronounced differently from English.

### 1. Final Devoicing: `b`, `d`, `g`
At the **start** of words and syllables, `b`, `d`, and `g` sound just like they do in English. However, at the **end** of a word or syllable (standing alone or in a consonant cluster), they "harden" (devoice):
* **`b` is pronounced as `[p]`**
* **`d` is pronounced as `[t]`**
* **`g` is pronounced as `[k]`** (or `[h]` after *i*, covered on Day 4)

Because of this rule, many differently spelled words become **homophones** (they sound identical!):

| Letter | Rule | Book Homophone / Sound-Alike Pairs | Example Sentence for Memory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **b $\rightarrow$ [p]** | End of word/syllable | **habt** (have) sounds like **klappt** (works out) | *Ihr **habt** Glück, es **klappt**!* (You have luck, it works!) |
| **d $\rightarrow$ [t]** | End of word/syllable | **wird** (becomes) sounds like **Wirt** (landlord)<br>**Rad** (wheel/bike) sounds like **Rat** (advice)<br>**Erdöl** (crude oil) sounds like *Ert-öl* | *Der **Wirt** gibt mir einen guten **Rat** für mein **Rad**.* (The landlord gives me good advice for my bike.) |
| **g $\rightarrow$ [k]** | End of word/syllable | **folg** (follow!) sounds like **Volk** (people)<br>**Krieg** (war) sounds like *kriek* | *Der **Tag** [tahk] war lang.* (The day was long.) |

### 2. Letters That Sound Different from English

| Letter(s) | Imitated Sound | Pronunciation Rule | Book Examples | Example Sentence for Memory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **c** | `[h]`, `[k]` | Rarely found on its own except in foreign words; principally used in combinations **ch** and **ck**. | *Café, Computer* | *Wir trinken Kaffee im **Café**.* (We drink coffee in the café.) |
| **j** | `[y]` | Almost always pronounced like the English **y** in *yet* or *young*. | **Junge** (boy), **Jammer** (misery/pity) | *Der **Junge** sagt **ja**.* (The boy says yes.) |
| **qu** | `[kv]` | Always followed by *u*, pronounced like **k + v** spoken in rapid succession. | **quer** (across/diagonal), **Quatsch** (nonsense), **Qualität** (quality) | *Red keinen **Quatsch**, die **Qualität** ist gut!* (Don't talk nonsense, the quality is good!) |
| **th** | `[t]` | **Never** like English 'th' in *this* or *thing*—always pronounced simply as **t**. Found in words of foreign origin. | **Apotheke** (pharmacy), **Hypothek** (mortgage), **Thymian** (thyme), **These** (thesis) | *Ich gehe in die **Apotheke** und ins **Theater**.* (I am going to the pharmacy and the theatre.) |
| **-tion** | `[tsiohn]` | Found in imported words ending in *-tion*. The *t* sounds like *ts*. | **Station** (station), **Aktion** (action/campaign), **Funktion** (function), **Tradition** (tradition) | *Das ist eine alte **Tradition** an dieser **Station**.* (That is an old tradition at this station.) |
| **v** | `[f]` | Almost always pronounced like **f** in *from* (except in some foreign loanwords like *Vase* where it sounds like *v*). | **Vater** (father), **von** (from/of), **Verlag** (publisher), **bevor** (before) | *Mein **Vater** hat **vier** Bücher **von** diesem **Verlag**.* (My father has four books from this publisher.) |
| **w** | `[v]` | Always pronounced like the English **v** in *very*. | **was** (what), **Wein** (wine), **Weg** (way/path), **Wirt** (landlord) | ***Was** trinkt der **Wirt**? **Wasser** oder **Wein**?* (What is the landlord drinking? Water or wine?) |
| **y** | `[ue]` | Pronounced as a vowel, sounding like the short German **ü** (rounded lips saying 'i'). | **System** (system), **sympathisch** (likeable/pleasant) | *Der Mann ist sehr **sympathisch**.* (The man is very likeable.) |
| **z** | `[ts]` | **NEVER** like the English *z* in *hazard*. Always like **t + s** spoken rapidly together (as in *Mozart*), even at the very start of a word! | **Zeit** (time), **zu** (to/closed), **Zinsen** (interest), **Zange** (pliers), **Zorn** (anger), **zusammen** (together), **beizeiten** (in good time), **inzwischen** (meanwhile), **Weizen** (wheat), **Schnauze** (snout), **Konzert** (concert), **Winzer** (winegrower), **Geiz** (avarice), **Sitz** (seat), **Franz**, **Holz** (wood), **Harz** (resin), **Lenz** (spring) | *Wir haben keine **Zeit**, wir gehen **zusammen** ins **Konzert**.* (We have no time, we are going together to the concert.) |

### 3. The Three Rules of `s` (`[z]`, `[s]`, and `[sh]`)

In German, the letter **s** behaves the exact opposite way to English depending on where it sits in a word:

#### A. `s` = `[z]` (like 'z' in *busy* / *is*)
Occurs at the **start of a word or syllable** (before a vowel) or between vowels:
* **Book Examples:** **sein** (to be / his), **Symbol** (symbol), **Absicht** (*Ab|sicht* – intention), **Fürsorge** (*Für|sorge* – welfare/care), **Rose** (*Ro|se* – rose), **Riese** (*Rie|se* – giant).
* **Example Sentence:** *Die **Rose** **ist** **sehr** schön.* (The rose is very beautiful.)

#### B. `s` = `[s]` (like 's' in *sits* / *its*)
Occurs at the **end of a word or syllable**:
* **Book Examples:** **Gast** (guest), **längst** (long since), **Wespe** (*Wes|pe* – wasp), **Muster** (*Mus|ter* – pattern), **meins** (mine), **meines** (*mei|nes* – of my).
* **Words containing BOTH `[z]` and `[s]` sounds:** **süß** (sweet $\rightarrow$ *zues*), **seins** (his $\rightarrow$ *zyns*), **seines** (*sei|nes* $\rightarrow$ *zy-nes*), **dieses** (*die|ses* – this $\rightarrow$ *dee-zes*).
* **Example Sentence:** ***Dieses** Eis ist **süß**, sagt der **Gast**.* (This ice cream is sweet, says the guest.)

#### C. `sp` and `st` = `[shp]` and `[sht]`
When **sp** or **st** appear at the **start of a word or syllable** (including after prefixes like *ge-*, *er-*, *ver-* or in compound words), the **s** is pronounced like the English **'sh'** (`[sh]`):
* **Book Examples (`sp`):** **Speck** (bacon), **spät** (late), **spülen** (to rinse/wash dishes), **gespannt** (*ge|spannt* – eager/curious).
* **Book Examples (`st`):** **Stamm** (tree trunk/tribe), **sterben** (to die), **Strecke** (route/distance), **steigen** (to climb/rise), **erstaunt** (*er|staunt* – astonished), **verstimmt** (*ver|stimmt* – out of tune/in a bad mood), **Anstand** (*An|stand* – decency).
* **Example Sentence:** *Es ist **spät**, wir **stehen** auf der **Straße**.* (It is late, we are standing on the street.)

## 1.3 Pronunciation: Consonant Combinations ('Blocks')

Unfamiliar groups of consonants can look intimidating at first, even when the individual sounds are easy. Learn these combinations as single phonetic **blocks**:

| Consonant Block | Imitated Sound | Pronunciation Rule | Book Examples | Example Sentence for Memory |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **dsch** | `[dj]` | Like the **j** in *jump*. Quite rare; used only in foreign loanwords. | **Dschungel** (jungle), **Dschihad**, **Dschunke** (junk boat) | *Der Tiger lebt im **Dschungel**.* (The tiger lives in the jungle.) |
| **gd** | `[kt]` | Like **kt** at the end of *flicked*. Fairly rare, though *Jagd* is common. | **Jagd** (hunt), **Magd** (maid) | *Der Hund geht auf die **Jagd**.* (The dog goes on the hunt.) |
| **hd, hl(t), hm(t), hn(t), hr(t), ht** | *(Silent h)* | This **h** is **not pronounced** at all! It simply signals that the preceding vowel is **long**. Ignore *h* between vowels too (except in compound words). | **Fehde** (feud), **Mehl** (flour), **wählt** (chooses/votes), **lahm** (lame), **rahmt** (frames), **kühn** (bold), **wohnt** (lives), **wahr** (true), **lehrt** (teaches), **weht** (blows), **sehen** (to see), **ruhen** (to rest).<br>*Compound exceptions (pronounce the h):* **Seehafen** (*See|hafen* – seaport), **bleihaltig** (*blei|haltig* – containing lead). | *Er **wohnt** hier und **geht** zum Bahnhof.* (He lives here and goes to the station.) |
| **ng** | `[ng]` | When between vowels, the *g* is barely vocalized, just like in the English word *singer* (never like *finger*!). | **Anger** (village green), **Finger** (finger – rhymes with *singer*!), **Hunger** (hunger) | *Ich habe großen **Hunger**.* (I am very hungry / I have great hunger.) |
| **pf** | `[pf]` | Spoken in rapid succession, almost simultaneously, whether at the start or end of a word/syllable (often with just a trace of the *p*). | **Pfeil** (arrow), **Pferd** (horse), **Pfund** (pound – 500g), **Dampfer** (steamer), **impfen** (to vaccinate), **Kupfer** (copper), **Sumpf** (swamp), **Krampf** (cramp), **glimpflich** (lightly/gently) | *Das **Pferd** kostet tausend **Pfund**.* (The horse costs a thousand pounds.) |
| **sch, schl, schm, schn, schr, schw** | `[sh]` | **sch** is always like English **'sh'** in *shut* or *wish*. Note that `sch` + consonant (`schl`, `schm`, `schn`, `schr`, `schw`) only occurs at the start of words. | **Schande** (shame), **Rausch** (intoxication/rush), **Schlampe** (slovenly person), **schmelzen** (to melt), **Schnauze** (snout/mouth), **Schraube** (screw), **schwitzen** (to sweat) | *Meine **Schwester** **schläft** schon.* (My sister is already sleeping.) |
| **tsch** | `[ch]` | Like English **'ch'** in *cheap* or *match*. Found mostly in the middle or at the end of words, occasionally at the start. | **tschüs** (bye!), **Tscheche** (Czech), **Matsch** (mud/slush), **futsch** (gone/ruined), **Deutschland** (Germany), **Rutsch** (slide/slip) | *Wir fahren nach **Deutschland**—**tschüs**!* (We are going to Germany—bye!) |
| **tz** | `[ts]` | Only occurs in the middle or at the end of words. Sounds just like *ts* in *gets* (identical to German **z**). | **plötzlich** (suddenly), **platzen** (to burst), **sitzen** (to sit), **Fritz**, **Gesetz** (law) | *Die **Katze** **sitzt** **plötzlich** auf dem Tisch.* (The cat is suddenly sitting on the table.) |
| **zw** | `[tsv]` | Like **t + s + v** spoken rapidly together. Occurs only at the start of words and syllables. | **Zwang** (compulsion), **bezwingen** (*be|zwingen* – to conquer), **Zwerg** (dwarf), **zwei** (two), **inzwischen** (*in|zwischen* – meanwhile), **Zweck** (purpose) | *Die **zwei** Kinder sitzen **zwischen** uns.* (The two children are sitting between us.) |

   *(Two boys are sitting in the car.)*

   *(The father lives in Germany.)*

   *(Late in the evening, the horse is hungry.)*

   *(This flour has good quality.)*

   *(Bye, see you later at the station!)*
